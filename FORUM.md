## BECKY I   AKA Ward beck project # 820322

_MY WBS CONSOLE · Oct 14, 2006|39_

> Ok folks, since this is my site and such and I've asked you all to post pictures of your consoles I felt it was fair that I posted some pictures of my own and give you a bit of a history about my console and my attachment to Ward Beck gear.. 
>
> My Name is Tony Kuzub, I'm a 23 year old recording engineer from Saskatoon Saskatchewan and I'm the biggest Ward Beck audio nut in the world ( I think). I've been recording and mixing music since the age of 10. My first exposure to professional audio was at my fathers work, a local television station. At the sound studio of CFQC TV in Saskatoon they had a stock L2042 that I cut my teeth on. (looking back knowing what I know now...it was bare bones at best) I learned everything on it...signal routing, patching and recording. Once my father and mother got divorced I no longer had access to the studio. So in a round about way my connection to ward beck reminds me of an un-broken home hahaha Anyways, they sold the console to someone else even after I offered to buy it from them. (the other matching ward beck in the plant was purchased through me by Len at bedside studio in Winnipeg) I was royally pissed off that my console got into the hands of a guy who took consoles apart and sold them on eBay. So, I decided from that day on that I would dedicate my time in preserving the consoles as a whole. So, I actively started looking for a console. I came across one at CBC in Regina and that's where the story starts:
>
> My console is Ward beck project # 820322 originally installed the 20th of September of 1982 (6 months before I was born) by Wai-Man Fung at CBC Studio 2 in Regina Saskatchewan. (I still have the original install test report) It's basically a stock L2404 with a several major mods. It has a huge patch bay, wider frame for more effects buckets, bigger remote, PPM's instead of VU meters and features a COMBined buss. The console also has other special mods done to it including a crazy monitoring matrix and phantom power for each channel. The monitoring matrix has 5 M498 modules that feed the monitors all the aux's and the subs.
>
> According the drawings the console was originally fitted with:
> (20) M470 Pre-amps
> (4) M462B
> (2) M466
> (2) M467
> and a custom made announcer turret linked to channels 19 and 20 (a big pain in the ass)
>
> but when I go it, the frame had been filled with 18 M462B's and the turret was gone
>
> The console was removed in April 2003
>
> My Friend Kevin helped me move the frame from Regina to Saskatoon in his truck. It rained the whole way. 
> The modules were moved earlier in a car...thank god. I had 6 friends help me move it from the truck, up to my 3rd floor studio. The console fit BARELY into the car and only had 3" of clearance. When it was in the hallway i was nearly crush as we had to turn it onto it's side. I've always said "you're not a real engineer unless you've almost been killed by a console"
>
> This is the console the night we moved it in. I had some mcCurdy faders to fill in where a custom monitoring panel rested. 
>
> Since the console was setup for broadcast, I had to add Direct outs to every channel. then normal them to the patchbay, then normal the outs to the digital recorder then later to my analog machine. In true ward beck fashion, all of the mods on my console were done using beldon cable. 
>
> After all the mods were done I started wiring in all the patch bay. 
> True story: A total of 300 channels of audio needed to be routed and configured. This was no small job for any studio owner. too boot, the space on either side of my console is under 10" and only 18" of space is left behind the console. Over 200 hours was spent wiring, soldering and building. I was dating this girl at the time I was installing the console, she thought I was cheating on her since I never saw her, because of Becky I lost that relationship. The joke ever since was my new girlfriend was 6'9", black and from Regina... and her name was Becky and I'm still with her. I think Len calls his reBecka
>
> Later I added more modules to my effects section. This is an old pictures, but at the moment I'm up to (10) M466 Compressors, (4) M464 Filters, (4)M462 Equalizers, (6)M467 Noise gates, (2) M466M meter modules and (7) M464A Equalizers. (20) M462B Equalizers, (4) M472 Equalizers, and (4) M522B Equalizers
>
> A lot of them are not in the console, but on a shelf ready to be swapped in when needed or when other modules have problems
>
> This was a custom cover I had made to cover the console when not in use. The cover is almost never on hahaha
>
> To accommodate my ever growing collection of outboard gear and pre-amps an additional 100 point patch bay was needed. I had bought a ward beck patch bay off the university of Saskatchewan and had it fitted into my frame. However, it did not terminate to anything so EACH point needed to be connected with snakes. Another 100 hour project. 
>
> To accommodate visiting engineers I needed to add patching on the front of my console. This is something I recommended everyone does. if you notice behind the grill there is a roll of cables. When I installed the console, I left about 6 feet of snake and cable for EVERY patch coiled up on this side of the console. Since My floor isn't floating, this was the best way to have some future flexibility.
>
> When I got My Pro Tools HD rig I decided to modify a few things, move a SHITLOAD of patch points (not a small task) and finally wire in my Studer A80 16 track 2" machine. If you look at the left side arm rest...yes I lost it while trying to have it re-covered. If you have one I will buy it from you! Right next to the Sub master faders I added a Dual Mono fader as a speaker level. I prefer this over the standard Potentiometers any day!

I think it's the coolest thing in the world. however....they are still patch bay labels see my other post about these labels for more in http://wbsps.kuzub.com/forum/index.php?topic=61.0

## Finally...

_MY WBS CONSOLE · Feb 27, 2008|41_

> I've had this frame for a while, and have acquired a pile of other parts as well. FINALLY I'm putting some of it together into something useful.
>
> I had picked up 2ea 470's and 2ea 470A's with the intent to rack them for pre-amps, and I found a 14ch broadcast console loaded with 490D's. I stole the console for next to nothing, not knowing anything about the 490 modules. Later on I bid on a lot of several 490-series modules along with other parts including a couple HP/LP filters & 3 meter bridges, thinking I might need the 490 parts - well, that lot ended up having several 497 dual masters, 2 HP/LP filters, 3 meter bridges, a few 490A's, 490B's, 490C's, 490D's, and about 20ea 490E's plus a few F's & 2ea 490EN's along with some switchers, monitor sends, 479 dual aux sends, 2ea oscillators, and a few other things.
>
> Oh, and I picked up a 480C for like $90 along the way, too, & racked it up... man, that one is sweet, no noise, every switch & pot perfect from the moment I received it. I've used it on acc guitar and snare, and I'd love to get another pair of these or some 460's to use on o/h's! (Tony, how does the 480C's eq compare with the 460's?)
>
> Okay, back to the desk...
>
> Here's how it looked when I got it...
>
> The console was set up 14ch line inputs and dual mono outputs for the radio station it came from, so I knew I'd have to make some changes. The desk also was pre-wired for 4 HP/LP inserts driven by 12/1 switchers. 3 of the switchers were bypassed with jumper cards, and 3 of the HP/LP slots were jumpered with bypass cards as well, provided from WBS, so these can simply be pulled and replaced with the appropriate modules. 2 channels did not allow for inserts. The desk was also pre-wired for all the 490-series modules, meaning cables were run to the tb's for stereo modules - Line A L&R in, Line B L&R in, so 4 input cables per module. The outputs were all bussed P1 L, P1 R, P2-L, P2-R, and feed to the 497's. There are 3 slots wired for 497's, and the 497 outputs wired down to the tb's. 
>
> Mods to the desk:
> Okay, so I was concerned about how to fix the fact that the output was configured for dual mono instead of stereo, but it turned out that WBS had plugged 2 cards into the extra 2 M497 slots that jumpered the L&R channels through resistors - so pulling those cards made it stereo out. Also wired was one Aux bus and the cue bus, feeding a 479 dual aux send and 3 monitor sends. Oh, and the oscillator with a custom-made switch (by the radio station I imagine) to feed the tone to the P1, P2 or Aux bus. As you can see, there's a VU meter for P1, P2 and a selectable one - I modified the selectable one to be fed by a 16/1 switcher and wired that off the insert of each channel so I can use the one meter for input sensitivity from each channel. Then made the meter selectable to be fed from the Aux bus, Cue bus, or the 'insert switcher'. That way I can use the cue send from any one module and drive the meter to see the post-fader output of any channel.
>
> The 2 columns just to the left of the input section had 2 M498 8x1 selectors in the top row and nothing in the bottom row. The 8x1's were feeding sat feeds for the radio station, so I eliminated them and modified the metal to install 2 more input channels, making it a 16ch desk.
>
> The right-hand door had the tb's with all the remote control wiring from the ribbon cables mounted to the back, and I don't need any of that so I pulled those tb's and drilled that panel for the i/o panel. I installed XLR's for inputs to each channel, XLR's for direct outs from each channel, and TRS send/receive for each channel insert, then Left out, Right out, Aux 1-L send, Aux 1-R send, and L&R returns that can also be used as direct inserts. (these will be fed through a 479 to the L/R bus). 
>
> The 490D's are a transformer line input with 2 selectable inputs & stereo output. I modified one module to use Line-A in as a mic input and Line-B in as a line input to test it and need to modify the rest since the test was successful.
>
> I used an oscillator with the +8dbm / 600-ohm & -70dbm / 150-ohm outputs to test the re-configured inputs. A&B are jumpered together so they share an XLR connector & switching from A to B changes the input from mic to line. The other junk on the table is an old Zoom 8ch 16-bit mixer I used to listen to the signal.
>
> So, that's my story so far... Yeah, I'm hooked. Thanks Tony for this great website, and all who have contributed! I'll put up more pictures as things progress.
>
> ~Brian
